People don't have a great vocabulary for conceptualizing a certain ontological
range of "humans, being". English compacts multiple semantic registers into the
word =being=, complicating our ability to express subjective aspects of human
experience.

#+begin_quote
Ambiguity and semantic overloading also makes this a difficult concept to teach
or transmit culturally. It's a complicated "domain" of knowledge, while it also
just =is= the copula.

~I think, therefore _I_ am.~ Does cartesian dualism really require two people? Obv.

=To think, therefore you are. No think, whereto? Not far=

Many languages have a similar problem clearly expressing a distinction between
the copula's gerund. French has a slightly different issue. The need to clarify
the ontological sense of =being= -- especially with precision or amidst various
layers of technical abstractions -- is arguably the product of philosophy ... or
perhaps philosophy's muse itself.

necessary pre-condition for consciousness nor is it sufficient to develop

Being does not require articulation in thought or in verbal communication to
exist. It just is, wherever/whoever it is.

At some point, various groups did identify it as a abstract object. Where
relevant to a specific language, a gerund:

+ may function as a nominalized verb: this is almost never a noun which

  - =swimming _can_ be hard. sometimes swimming hurts, but swimming never steals,

+ may modify verbs (via conjugation)

The gerund =be-ing= of its copula (the =be-ing= of it's =to-be=) could mean:

+ Some =noun= with capacity for =subjective experience= _is experiencing_ something.
  That definition uses an intransitive conjugation (passive) -- i.e. they are
  in the continued state of [simply] having something happen to them. This
  sense is somewhat uncommon: =being's= ambiguity muddles the interpretation
  further until we disregard the difference.
+ Some =noun= with capacity for =agency= is =be-ing=.
  - "How =noun= feels or does affects how =sujet= chooses or feels."
  - "When =noun= is being voiced in an ironically passive sense, it's so
    irritating. =noun= is always =be-ing= that way on purpose."
  - "=be-ing= is always being like that" only makes sense in a philosophical
    context. a =Being= can have agency, but what about =be-ing=, the process.
+ Some =noun= is =is-ing= (continually existing in a state of definedness). That
  sounds weird because it _is_.
